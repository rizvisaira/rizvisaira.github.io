Saira Rizvi

Staff Data Scientist & Manager, Security Insights & AI, RBC

I am a Staff Data Scientist and Manager on the Security Insights & AI team at RBC in Toronto, where I lead applied machine learning for fraud, phishing, and behavioural threat detection. I hold an M.Sc. in Data Science from the University of Waterloo, advised by Prof. Charles L. A. Clarke, and an Honours B.Sc. in Mathematics and Statistics from the University of Toronto.

My work is concerned with detecting adversarial behaviour in deployed systems: phishing campaigns, coordinated abuse, and fraudulent account activity, all evaluated against opponents who adapt in response to detection. I am particularly interested in signals that survive that adaptation — structural and behavioural patterns that hold when content-level features stop being reliable — and in the failures that occur at the decision boundary, where a system misjudges what a user is actually trying to do.

I am increasingly interested in AI safety and risk, particularly the assessment of deployed language models and open-ended agents: how misread intent produces both over-refusal and unsafe compliance, what bounded personalization could contribute as a safety mechanism, and how threat-actor profiling methods might be adapted to characterise agent behaviour. My earlier research was on misinformation retrieval and evaluation.

Research Interests

Bounded personalization as a safety mechanism

Alignment to aggregated preference washes out small populations, and misuse tends to originate in exactly such a population. Inferring what a particular user is trying to do, within explicit bounds, may reduce misfires in both directions — fewer refusals for legitimate specialist use, fewer completions for adversarial use that reads as ordinary. The open question is where the bound belongs, since unbounded personalization is itself an attack surface.

Behavioural characterization of open-ended agents

Threat intelligence profiles adversaries by what they repeatedly do rather than by what they claim. Agents acting over long horizons may be best understood the same way, through observed patterns across many episodes rather than single-response evaluation.

Misinformation retrieval and evaluation

Generative models changed the economics of producing misinformation without changing the retrieval problem underneath. I remain interested in what it means for a system to be correct about a contested claim, and how that judgement is made reproducible.
